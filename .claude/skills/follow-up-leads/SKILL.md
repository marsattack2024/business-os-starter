# Follow Up on Leads

Most money is lost to silence, not to "no." This writes the messages that break the silence.

## Read first

- `context/rules.md` — what this business may never claim or do. These rules beat anything below.
- `context/voice.md` — these are personal messages, not marketing
- `context/offer.md` — the exact offer and price they were quoted
- `context/customers.md` — why people hesitate

## Steps

1. Ask the owner for the situation. One message, three questions:
   - Who went quiet, and what was the last thing that happened?
   - How long ago?
   - Is there anything new you could tell them today? (a slot opened, a price holds until X, you finished a similar job)

   If they have a list of several, ask them to paste it and handle each one.

2. Write a **sequence of three**, spaced out:
   - **Message 1 (now)** — short, warm, no pressure, one specific question that's easy to answer
   - **Message 2 (four days later)** — bring something new: a relevant example, a piece of proof, a deadline that's actually real
   - **Message 3 (ten days later)** — the close-the-loop message. "I'll stop bugging you — want me to keep the slot or let it go?" This one gets the most replies.

3. Rules that hold every time:
   - Never open with "just checking in" or "following up." Say why you're writing.
   - No guilt, no fake urgency, no invented deadline. A deadline that isn't real costs the owner their reputation.
   - Each message readable in ten seconds.
   - Give them an easy way to say no. It's what makes saying yes safe.

4. Show all three at once so the owner sees the arc.

5. Tell them plainly: these get sent by hand unless they've connected an email tool. Point at `connect-accounts` if they want it automated later.

## Save it

`content/YYYY-MM-DD-followup-<who>.md` — all three messages with the send dates written at the top.

## Done when

- Three messages exist, each with a date to send it.
- Every fact — price, timing, availability — came from the owner or `context/offer.md`.
- Nothing was sent by you.
