# Teaching contract

This skill adapts the learning-design argument in Geoffrey Litt's talk
[Understanding is the new bottleneck](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WkBPX-oDMnA)
to Business OS. Its job is to reduce cognitive debt, including cognitive debt
created by over-explaining.

## Governing principles

1. **Match the artifact to the next job.** Approval needs a decision brief.
   Operation and onboarding need training. More sections are not automatically
   more understanding.
2. **State the decision before the curriculum.** An owner should know the
   recommendation, current behavior, proposed change, and proof required in the
   first viewport.
3. **Define each core idea once.** Repetition is justified only when the second
   appearance asks the learner to apply the idea in a new situation.
4. **Make relationships spatial.** A visual should reveal boundaries, flow,
   mapping, comparison, or state. A row of text tabs is progressive disclosure,
   not an illustration.
5. **Use literate causal order.** Introduce why evidence matters before showing
   it. Follow behavior, not alphabetical filenames.
6. **Use retrieval practice selectively.** A short quiz is valuable for
   training and distracting in most approval briefs.
7. **Preserve epistemic boundaries.** Distinguish evidence, proposal,
   inference, and unknowns. Security metaphors never replace trust-boundary
   proof.
8. **Keep microworlds bounded.** Visual simulations are disposable,
   deterministic teaching tools and never mutate real systems.

## Participation tests

### Decision brief

A careful owner can answer:

- What exactly am I deciding?
- What exists today and what would change?
- Which proof must pass before approval?
- What risk or trust boundary remains unresolved?

### Training pack

A careful learner can answer:

- What constraint shaped the design?
- What happens before, during, and after the representative path?
- Which input or state change alters the result?
- What stays unchanged?
- Where would I safely make the next related change?

## Personalization without ceremony

Infer the reader's baseline from the request. Translate implementation into
business consequences for an owner or employee. Use implementation vocabulary
for engineers without abandoning the causal model. Avoid internal trivia in a
client-facing pack.

Background should be skimmable, but it is not mandatory in a decision brief
when the reader already supplied the needed context.
