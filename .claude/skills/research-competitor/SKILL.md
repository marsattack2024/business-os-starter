# Research a Competitor

What they charge, what they promise, where they're weak. Facts the owner can act on, not a personality assessment.

## Read first

- `context/offer.md` — what we charge, so the comparison means something
- `context/customers.md` — who we're both fighting over
- `context/gtm.md` — who we already decided to be (skip if empty)

## Steps

1. Ask who, if the owner hasn't said. A name or a website is enough. If they say "I don't know who my competitors are," find three by searching what their customers would search.

2. Go look. Their website, their pricing page, their socials, their reviews — especially the **bad** reviews, which say more than anything on their own site.

3. Write the brief with these five parts:
   - **What they sell and what it costs** — real prices where findable. If pricing is hidden, say "not published" rather than guessing.
   - **What they promise** — the headline claim, in their own words
   - **Who they're for** — and who they're clearly not for
   - **What customers complain about** — quotes from real reviews. This is the most valuable section; spend the most time here.
   - **The gap** — what they don't offer, don't say, or do badly, that the owner could own

4. End with **three moves** the owner could make this month. Concrete: a price change, a page to write, a promise to make that this competitor can't.

5. Never copy their copy. Point at the gap and write into it — that's how the owner ends up different rather than second.

6. Mark anything uncertain with "guess:" in front. Prices and claims change; a stale fact repeated confidently is worse than no fact.

## Save it

`content/YYYY-MM-DD-competitor-<name>.md`

## Done when

- The complaints section quotes real reviews, not summaries.
- The three moves are things the owner could start this week.
- Everything unverified is marked as a guess.
