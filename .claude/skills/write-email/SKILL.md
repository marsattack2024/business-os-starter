# Write an Email

## Read first

- `context/rules.md` — what this business may never claim or do. These rules beat anything below.
- `context/voice.md` — read the sample twice before writing a word. Match its sentence length, its warmth, its punctuation. This is the whole job.
- `context/offer.md` — if the email mentions anything they sell or any price
- `context/customers.md` — if it's going to a customer or a prospect

## Steps

1. Get three things. Ask for whatever the owner didn't already say, in one short message:
   - Who is this going to, and what's your history with them?
   - What do you want to happen after they read it?
   - Anything they need to know that you know and I don't?

2. Write the email. Rules that hold every time:
   - Subject line under 8 words, says the actual thing
   - First line is about them, not about us
   - One ask, near the end, stated plainly
   - Prices exactly as they appear in `context/offer.md`. If a price isn't in that file, ask — never estimate one.
   - No sign-off flourishes the owner wouldn't say out loud

3. Read it back against `context/voice.md`. If a sentence sounds more polished than the owner's sample, rewrite it rougher. Sounding like them beats sounding good.

4. Show the owner the draft in the chat. Say in one sentence what you were going for.

5. They approve, they change it, or they scrap it. If they change wording, look at what they changed — if it's a pattern, add it to `context/voice.md` in the same turn and tell them you did.

## Save it

`content/YYYY-MM-DD-email-<who-its-to>.md` — for example `content/2026-08-22-email-maria-follow-up.md`.

## Done when

- The draft is saved in `content/`.
- The owner has read it and said send it, or told you what to change.
- You did not send anything. The owner sends it themselves.
