# Ask For Reviews

The cheapest growth a local business has. Most owners never ask, or ask badly.

## Read first

- `context/rules.md` — what this business may never claim or do. These rules beat anything below.
- `context/voice.md` — this is the owner writing to someone who likes them
- `context/customers.md` — what customers already say
- `context/offer.md` — what they bought

## Steps

1. Ask three things:
   - Who are the happy ones? (names, or "the last ten customers")
   - Where do you want the review — Google, Facebook, somewhere else?
   - Do you have the direct link to leave one?

   No link? Tell them how to get it: search their own business name on Google, click "Write a review," copy the address. One sentence, no more.

2. Write **two versions**, because different customers answer different channels:
   - **Text message** — under 40 words, first names, the link, one sentence of why it helps
   - **Email** — five sentences, mentions the specific thing they bought

3. Rules that hold every time:
   - Ask within days of the good moment. Say this to the owner if they're chasing customers from last year.
   - Name the specific job. "Thanks for your business" gets ignored; "your kitchen came out great" gets a review.
   - Make the link impossible to miss.
   - Never suggest what to write, never offer anything in exchange. Both break the platform's rules and both are obvious to readers.

4. If the owner names specific customers, write a personal version for each rather than one template with a name swapped in.

5. Add a line to `context/goals.md` suggestion pool: ask for a review after every finished job. That's the habit that compounds.

## Save it

`content/YYYY-MM-DD-review-requests.md` — both versions, plus any personalized ones, plus the review link.

## Done when

- A text version and an email version both exist.
- The review link is in the file, not "insert link here."
- The owner knows who to send it to and when.
