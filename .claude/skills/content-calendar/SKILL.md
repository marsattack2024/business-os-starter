# Content Calendar

Removes the daily "what do I post" decision. Plan once, then `write-content` does the making.

## Read first

- `context/rules.md` — what this business may never claim or do. These rules beat anything below.
- `context/gtm.md` — the angles worth repeating. Empty? Run `launch-gtm` first.
- `context/customers.md` — the questions they ask
- `context/offer.md` — what we're actually selling
- `context/goals.md` — what this month should move
- The last two weeks of `content/` — so the plan doesn't repeat what just went out

## Steps

1. Ask two things:
   - How many pieces a week can you realistically do? (Take the lower number if they hesitate. A plan they abandon in week two is worse than three posts a week they keep.)
   - Which platforms are actually yours?

2. Build the plan around **four recurring types**, rotated:
   - **Answer** — a real question customers ask
   - **Proof** — a job, a result, a before-and-after
   - **Opinion** — something the owner believes that competitors won't say
   - **Offer** — what to buy and why now (roughly one in five, no more)

3. Write out four weeks. Each entry is one line: date, platform, type, and the actual working title or hook. Not "post about services" — "Why we don't quote over the phone."

4. Mark the two or three pieces that matter most. If the owner only does three things this month, these are them.

5. Pull ideas from the owner's real life: jobs they're doing, questions they got this week, things they complained about. Ask for those directly — it's the difference between a plan they use and a plan that looks nice.

6. Offer to write the first one now with `write-content`.

## Save it

`content/YYYY-MM-DD-content-calendar.md` — the four weeks, plus a short list of leftover ideas for next time.

## Done when

- Every entry has a real working title, not a topic area.
- The volume matches what the owner said they can do.
- The first piece is either written or scheduled to be.
