# Quality Loop

For when a draft is fine but not good. Works on anything — a page, an email, an offer, a tool. The trick is deciding what "good" means before judging it.

## Read first

- The thing being improved
- `context/voice.md` and `context/customers.md` — the standard is usually about the reader, not about writing

## Steps

1. **Agree the standard first.** Ask the owner: "what would make this a 10 for you?" Turn their answer into **five specific checks** — things you can genuinely say pass or fail. Show the five and get a yes before touching anything.

   Weak check: "is it engaging?" Strong check: "would a customer know the price and what to do next within 10 seconds?"

2. **Score it honestly.** Go through all five. For each: pass, fail, and one line of why. Give it a number out of 5.

   Being generous here wastes everyone's time. If it's a 2, say 2.

3. **Fix the lowest score only.** One thing per round. Fixing everything at once means nobody knows what worked.

4. **Score again**, all five, same checks. Say what changed.

5. Repeat until every check passes, or until two rounds in a row don't improve the score. If the score stalls, stop and say so: "this has stopped getting better — the problem is the standard or the underlying idea, not the writing." That's a real finding, not a failure.

6. Show the owner the first version and the last version side by side. The improvement is the point, and it isn't visible without the before.

## Save it

`content/YYYY-MM-DD-quality-loop-<what>.md` — the five checks, the score each round, and the final version.

## Done when

- Five checks were agreed before any editing happened.
- Every round has a real score, including the honest low ones.
- Either everything passes, or you said plainly why it stopped improving.
