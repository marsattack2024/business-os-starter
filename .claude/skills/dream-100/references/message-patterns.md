# Message patterns

These are structures, not fill-in-and-send templates. Replace every bracketed claim with verified, target-specific evidence. If a claim is not proven, omit it.

## Core pattern

1. **Observed fit:** one current detail that explains why this target belongs on the list.
2. **Relevant identity:** who the sender is and the audience/problem they serve.
3. **Value first:** one useful, concrete offer.
4. **Small next step:** a question that can be answered without booking a meeting.
5. **Easy exit:** no pressure and no manufactured urgency.

## Creator or influencer collaboration

> Hi [Name] — your recent [specific post/project] reached exactly the [audience/problem] we work with in [place/category].
>
> I run [business/show/community], serving [verified audience description; include numbers only if confirmed]. I have an idea for [specific asset or experience] that would give your audience [specific benefit] and give you [usable content/exposure/resource].
>
> Would it be useful if I sent the two-sentence concept? No pressure if it is not a fit.

## Pod Photography example

> Hi [Name] — I saw your [specific post, business, or recent project], and the overlap with women in the Miami community we serve stood out.
>
> I run The Pod Photography and [verified podcast/community/email description]. I have an idea for a collaboration where we create [specific content or experience] and introduce your work to our audience in a way that is genuinely useful to them.
>
> Would you like me to send the idea? Totally fine if the timing is not right.

Only add figures such as “4,000 members” or “6,000 email subscribers” after checking the current source. Do not copy numbers from an old message or memory.

## Complimentary experience

Use one of two honest models:

**True gift, no endorsement obligation**

> I would like to offer you [specific experience/product], normally valued at [verified value], with no posting or endorsement required. If you enjoy it and later want to discuss a collaboration, we can do that separately.

**Partnership with deliverables**

> I would like to propose a partnership: we provide [specific value], and you provide [specific deliverable, timing, usage rights]. Any public content would clearly disclose the relationship. Would you like the one-page terms?

Never call the second model “no strings attached.”

## Referral or strategic partner

> Your work with [specific shared audience/use case] looks complementary to ours. We often meet people who need [their relevant service/resource].
>
> I put together [small useful resource or referral idea] that could help both audiences without forcing a formal partnership.
>
> Is [business contact/channel] the right place to send it?

## Buyer or account

> I noticed [specific current signal] and did a short review of [relevant problem]. One issue/opportunity stood out: [specific, non-sensitive observation].
>
> I can send the two findings and what I would test first. No deck or sales call required.
>
> Would that be useful?

After a reply, run discovery before prescribing a full solution. Confirm need, impact, timing, authority, current alternatives, and what a useful next step would be.

## Follow-up pattern

A follow-up must add something new:

- a sharper idea;
- a relevant asset or introduction;
- a clarified low-friction option;
- a factual answer to their concern;
- a close-the-loop note.

Do not send “just bumping this,” copy the original message into a new channel, or use guilt, false scarcity, fake familiarity, or a calendar link before interest exists.

## Review checklist

- Is the first line unique to this target?
- Is every number and claim verified?
- Would they still receive value if they decline?
- Are compensation, deliverables, disclosure, and usage rights honest?
- Is the ask smaller than the relationship warrants?
- Does the message sound like the sender?
- Is “no” easy and respected?
