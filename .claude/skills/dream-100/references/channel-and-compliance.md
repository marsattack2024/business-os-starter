# Channel and compliance

Reviewed 2026-08-19. Recheck current law, platform rules, provider requirements, and local business policy before a live campaign. This is an operational safety baseline, not legal advice.

## The channel map

Choose the least intrusive professional path that fits the relationship.

| Channel | Appropriate use | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|
| Public engagement | Genuine, relevant contribution with no hidden pitch | That repeated likes/comments create consent |
| Social DM | A short relevant message where platform rules and context permit it | That a public profile invites commercial repetition |
| Business email | A published professional address with a documented lawful basis and compliant message | That a published address is blanket consent everywhere |
| Website form | A form explicitly intended for partnerships, press, vendors, or general business inquiries | That support, booking, intake, or personal forms are prospecting channels |
| Phone | A legitimate business call after preference/DNC and jurisdiction checks | That a public number permits automated calls or repeated solicitation |
| SMS/text | Only with a confirmed lawful basis or consent and an approved provider workflow | That a public business number is text consent |
| Physical mail | A lawful, appropriate high-value touch with accurate sender identity | That a package creates an endorsement obligation |
| Paid sponsorship/ads | Transparent paid access with spend and creative approval | That paid access creates an undisclosed organic relationship |

The presence of six possible channels does not mean use all six. Start with the strongest appropriate professional path. Add another only when a documented reason, new value, genuine trigger, current rules, and relationship context support it. Never use simultaneous duplicated pitches or personal-channel escalation to overcome silence.

## Suppression rules

Immediately set `SUPPRESSED` at the appropriate contact, account, or channel scope and stop covered outreach when there is:

- an explicit opt-out or do-not-contact request;
- a do-not-contact request or complaint;
- an applicable suppression/DNC record;
- a platform warning or account restriction;
- uncertain authority or lawful basis that cannot be resolved;
- a sensitive, vulnerable, or personal context where outreach would be inappropriate.

Use `DECLINED` or `CLOSED` for a no to the current offer, and `WRONG_CONTACT` or `WRONG_FIT` for a mismatched person or audience. These states stop the current campaign and never justify immediate channel escalation, but they are distinct from a binding opt-out. Do not reinterpret “not interested,” “stop,” or silence as permission to keep pitching. Public content may still be read normally; do not use engagement as a disguised continuation of the pitch.

## US email baseline

FTC CAN-SPAM applies to commercial email, including B2B. Use accurate sender/header information, a non-deceptive subject, required ad identification where applicable, a valid physical postal address, a clear opt-out, and timely opt-out fulfillment. Outsourcing the send does not remove responsibility.

Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business

## Gifts and endorsements

Free or discounted products/services, payments, affiliate arrangements, employment/family ties, and other material relationships may require clear and conspicuous disclosure with the endorsement. Platform disclosure tools alone may not be enough.

Sources:

- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers
- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews

## Phone, text, and global campaigns

- Do not automate or bulk-send cold SMS or prerecorded/automated marketing calls from this skill.
- Check DNC, consent, time-of-day, caller identity, and jurisdiction requirements before live phone/SMS work.
- For Canada, the UK/EU, Australia, or another jurisdiction, do not reuse a US email assumption. Determine the local lawful basis, notice, consent, identification, and opt-out rules first.

Primary starting points:

- US telemarketing: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule
- UK B2B marketing: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
- GDPR right to object: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/art_21/oj
- Canada CASL: https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm
- Australia spam rules: https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam

## Platform behavior

Never use unauthorized scraping, fake accounts, credential sharing, synthetic engagement, automated connection requests, or automated DMs. Recheck the target platform's current rules before using automation.

Official starting points:

- LinkedIn automated activity: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387/prohibited-software-and-extensions
- LinkedIn community policies: https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies
- Instagram spam guidance: https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/477434105621119
- Facebook spam guidance: https://www.facebook.com/help/152457281489359

## Approval boundary

Automation may assist with public-source research, deduplication, scoring, reminders, suppression enforcement, draft generation, and reporting. Human/provider authority remains necessary for:

- final fit and personalization judgment;
- live sends and channel changes;
- consent or lawful-basis decisions;
- gifts, spend, contracts, affiliate terms, deliverables, and usage rights;
- interpreting objections or sensitive replies;
- public posting and endorsements.
